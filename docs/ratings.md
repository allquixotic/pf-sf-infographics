# Reading and contributing ratings

These charts introduce classes and help start a conversation about character choice. They are not tier lists,
DPR simulations, popularity polls, or evidence of community consensus. A lower score does not mean a class is
unfun or unsuitable. A class family describes its usual approach; martial classes can still have magical abilities.

Choose **Revised role estimates** or **Original ratings (September 2026)** in the app's single Rating set dropdown.
The circled question mark explains the selected perspective. **About these ratings** gives the rubric and each
class's source links, rules version, review date and reasoning. Alternative sets can have entirely different class
rosters and details; see [how to add one](content-format.md#multiple-rating-sets-in-one-repository).

## Revised role estimates

The October 5, 2026 revision assumes ordinary viable builds under standard rules, without Free Archetype or
other optional power increases. It considers early (1–4), middle (5–12), and late (13–20) play qualitatively;
these are broad career estimates, not level-by-level measurements or a numerical average. Party composition,
encounters, daily resources, equipment and player decisions can change outcomes substantially. Scores compare
classes within their own game, not Pathfinder against Starfinder. Playtest scores remain provisional.

For offense, defense, support and utility: **0** = no meaningful native tools, **1** = limited,
**2** = situational or secondary, **3** = capable, **4** = strong, **5** = a defining specialty.
Half-points refine this editorial scale; they do not imply measured precision.

| Metric | What it includes |
| --- | --- |
| Offense | Direct single-target or area damage, burst and sustained damage. A specialization can excel without doing all four equally well. Setup and resource costs matter. |
| Defense | Personal resilience through AC, saves, HP, resistances and mitigation. Protecting another character belongs under support. |
| Support | Healing, protection, buffs, debuffs, control and actions or damage enabled for allies. This credits Commander and Guardian contributions even when their own damage is modest. |
| Utility | Noncombat problem solving through skills, exploration abilities and spells. Preparing the right spell is still a real constraint. |
| Complexity | Preparation, resources to track, tactical sequencing and rules knowledge. Higher is more to manage, not better or worse. |

Complexity anchors: **0** = almost no class-specific decisions; **1** = straightforward routine;
**2** = a small toolkit; **3** = recurring tactical or resource choices; **4** = substantial sequencing,
preparation or coordination; **5** = several interacting systems with high planning demands.
The UI's maximum-complexity filter uses the **upper endpoint** of a range and excludes classes with no score.
It is a convenience filter, not a recommendation about who is capable of playing a class.

Solid squares show a baseline; hatching shows a **build-dependent range**. Endpoints describe different plausible
specializations. They are not uncertainty bars, minimum and maximum theoretical optimization, or a promise that
one build reaches every upper endpoint at once. Broad spell lists and option-heavy classes particularly depend
on their chosen toolkit. Read the per-axis rationale before treating a range as a comparison.

## Original ratings

This set retains the project's September 2026 numerical scores. It uses corrected current summaries, sources,
family names and the Complexity label so historical comparisons do not reintroduce rules mistakes. Its own
legend explicitly says that the scores predate the revised rubric. It does not recreate every aspect of the old
poster and should not be cited as community agreement.

## Corrections and other perspectives

Use **Suggest a correction** in the app or the
[content correction form](https://github.com/allquixotic/pf-sf-infographics/issues/new?template=content-correction.yml).
Include the game, set, class, rules version, current claim and proposed change. Rules links substantiate mechanics;
the numerical interpretation still needs an explanation. For a rating, concrete builds and level bands are more
useful than an unsupported replacement number.

Contributors can offer a clearly named perspective with its own description, assumptions, complete roster and
per-class reasoning. Permission and attribution are required before importing someone else's chart or data.
Community feedback informed this revision, including requests for clearer selection, non-hierarchical class
families, Remaster corrections and recognition of area damage and indirect support. No commenter has been
represented as endorsing the final scores.

The source of this round of feedback is the
[Updated infographics discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/Pathfinder2e/comments/1wxw8ra/updated_infographics/).
[Classfinder](https://classfinder2e.com/) and the
[community class overview](https://www.reddit.com/r/Pathfinder2e/comments/1tgcjrb/updated_pf2e_classes_chart/)
are additional ways to explore class choices. Their categories and assumptions differ; these scores were not
copied from either resource.

Mechanic and Technomancer remain explicitly labeled as the **Tech Class Playtest**, with the playtest and errata
linked in their reviews. This revision does not claim to summarize the final Tech Core classes. Luminary,
Daredevil and Slayer also remain marked as playtest material and are hidden by default.
